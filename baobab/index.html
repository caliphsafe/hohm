SENEGALESE HERITAGE

Bouye belongs
at the table.

Baobab is known as bouye in Wolof. Its pale, naturally dry fruit pulp becomes a tangy, creamy drink when mixed with water.

In Senegal, the fruit is enjoyed in drinks and other foods. Families prepare it by breaking the shell, soaking or mixing the pulp in water, removing seeds and coarse fibers, then sweetening to taste. Some versions include milk or flavorings; HOHM’s proposed original uses a dairy-free base.

A fruit nectar, ready to enjoy.

Open. Shake. Sip. The consumer receives a finished drink—no mixing or preparation required.

Baobab naturally dries within its shell. Food-grade separated pulp is the fruit input; it is blended and processed into the bottled beverage before sale.

Cissé et al., 2009 · Senegalese fruit and nectar processing. The proposed product is a fruit drink/nectar, not a claim of 100% undiluted juice.

PRODUCT & PROCESS

A repeatable bottle

NUTRITION & WELLNESS

Small daily ritual.
Substantial fruit story.

Vitamin C & immune function

Vitamin C helps normal immune function. Baobab’s vitamin C content makes this a meaningful formulation priority; adequate intake supports normal function, rather than guaranteeing fewer illnesses.

Collagen formation

Vitamin C is needed to produce collagen, supporting connective tissue and normal wound healing. This is an established nutrient function, not an anti-aging or skin-transformation promise.

Plant-based iron absorption

Vitamin C improves absorption of nonheme iron from foods. Bouye can fit naturally alongside plant-based meals; it should not be positioned as an anemia treatment.

Digestive fiber

Baobab pulp contains soluble and insoluble dietary fiber. Retaining edible pulp creates a route to meaningful fiber in a drink, supporting daily fiber intake and normal bowel function.

Antioxidant nutrition

Vitamin C has a physiological antioxidant role. Baobab also contains polyphenols; their laboratory antioxidant activity supports ingredient research, not a claim to prevent disease.

Gut microbiome potential

A 2021 in-vitro fermentation study found prebiotic potential in pectin-rich baobab pulp. It supports further product research; it does not establish a gut-health benefit in people drinking HOHM.

Nutrient roles: NIH · Vitamin C functions and stability. Fruit composition: Monteiro et al., 2022 · Fruit-pulp composition. Microbiome research: 2021 laboratory study · Prebiotic potential. Benefits depend on the amount retained in the finished beverage and the overall diet.

POSITIONING & ROUTE TO MARKET

Win a focused shelf.
Earn the next one.

Start where the story connects

African and diaspora grocers, independent cafés, and selected wellness-oriented food shops in one delivery region. Pair the cultural story with chilled sampling.

Sell the drinking experience

Lead with creamy texture and citrus tang. Explain the fruit and the reason to retain its pulp. Make repeat purchase—not a one-time novelty—the goal.

Expand after repeat orders

Track bottles per store per week, repeat orders, spoilage, and delivery cost. Add accounts only when reorder economics support the next route.

Existing brands already sell prepared baobab beverages: Sip · Ready-to-drink baobab lists multiple bottle and pack formats, with a displayed starting price of $19.96. This validates category presence, not HOHM demand or a directly comparable per-bottle price.

HOHM’s proposed $5.99 shelf price is a premium positioning decision to test with buyers and consumers. No market-share claim or signed retailer is assumed.
